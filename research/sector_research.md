# Sector Research — Industrial Goods Manufacturing (Procurement Function)

**Sector:** Industrial Goods Manufacturing — Capital Equipment / Heavy Machinery
**Company size:** Large (multi-plant, multi-country operations; $1B+ revenue range)

## Company snapshot for this pitch
Chleo runs a large industrial equipment manufacturer — machinery, components, or systems sold to other manufacturers (B2B capital goods). Multiple plants, a fragmented supplier base built up over years of acquisitions and regional sourcing decisions, and a procurement function that's grown reactively rather than by design. This is a deliberately common profile — most large industrial manufacturers look exactly like this.

## Market context: why now
Procurement is not a peripheral function in manufacturing — it's the function. 60–70% of the costs manufacturing companies incur run through procurement, and manufacturing and automotive is the single largest end-use segment in the global procurement software market.

Yet the function is strikingly behind on AI relative to its own weight. Manufacturing sits at 58% of large enterprises running AI in production, trailing technology (94%), financial services (87%), and professional services (81%). Inside procurement specifically, the gap widens further: the industry averages just 2.1 out of 5 on AI readiness, with AI maturity itself the lowest-scoring dimension at 1.8, and only 8% of procurement teams have moved past pilots into real deployment.

Where organizations have deployed AI in this space, the returns are concrete: AI-enabled procurement and distribution operations see a 5–15% reduction in procurement spend, and companies with AI-mature supply chains run 23% more profitable than their peers.

## Why this company, why now
Three forces are converging on large industrial manufacturers right now:
1. **Input cost volatility** — commodity and component price swings have made "what should this actually cost" a live question procurement can't answer fast enough manually
2. **Supply chain diversification pressure** — geopolitical and tariff shifts are pushing companies to re-examine supplier concentration, and most don't have clean visibility into where that concentration actually sits
3. **Data exists, but it's unusable** — most large manufacturers have years of PO/invoice data sitting in ERP systems, but it's messy, inconsistently categorized, and effectively invisible to leadership
