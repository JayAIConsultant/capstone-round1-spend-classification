# Opportunities and Risks — AI in Spend Management

## Opportunities

| Opportunity | Why it matters to Chleo |
|---|---|
| **Spend visibility** | You can't negotiate, consolidate, or control what you can't see. Most large manufacturers can't answer "how much do we actually spend on X, across all plants" without weeks of manual pulling. |
| **Supplier concentration & fragmentation insight** | Fragmented spend = both risk (too many small suppliers, inconsistent quality/terms) and missed leverage (too much spend split when it could be consolidated for better pricing) |
| **Contract compliance / maverick spend recovery** | Spend that bypasses negotiated contracts is pure margin leakage — often invisible until someone looks for it specifically |
| **Faster, more consistent category structure** | Manual spend classification is slow, inconsistent between analysts, and rarely kept current — automating it makes every downstream procurement decision faster |

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation angle |
|---|---|
| **AI misclassifies spend, leading to bad decisions** | Human-in-the-loop review for low-confidence classifications; transparency into reasoning (this is exactly what the LangSmith sample demonstrates) |
| **Data quality / inconsistent source records** | Start with a defined data quality baseline; treat "unclassifiable" as a valid, flagged output rather than forcing a guess |
| **Change management — procurement team distrust of automation** | Position as augmentation (surfacing insight faster) not replacement; involve procurement analysts in reviewing early outputs |
| **Over-reliance on AI without governance** | Clear audit trail per classification decision; periodic human spot-checks built into the workflow |
| **Compliance exposure if used for higher-stakes decisions later** | Keep initial scope explicitly to spend visibility/classification — not supplier scoring that could affect contract/legal outcomes without further review (relevant for EU AI Act framing in Round 2) |
